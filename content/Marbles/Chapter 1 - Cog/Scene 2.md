Before I knew, her eyes reached for something etched deep inside me. Those eyes drew an emotion out of me I couldn't quite describe, suppressing the train of the cheap fantasy ramming through my brain. It was the first time I'd seen her, yet that gaze was strangely familiar. Too familiar.

She looked away shortly after. The face mask made her expression unreadable.

A silent sound escaped my lips. None could tell if it'd been a laugh or a sigh. I tried holding onto the thoughts.

The dirty fantasy roamed free again in my head. Had she not averted her gaze, she could've made me forget it.

There is no bell in our college for when periods end. At least I don't hear one. I keep looking at the four digits on my feature phone, eager to leave.

I left the classroom behind with as much eagerness. She was still packing up. 

An incredible urge to get some words onto the page for my novel had me overwhelmed. *Which one?* I thought. *This one? Too complicated to start yet. That one?* The mere length I imagined was enough for me to give up the next moment.

*Maybe I'll just pick up the short story instead and write a page.*

A painful laugh echoed through my mind, knowing full well I wouldn't. I would just sit there and aimlessly move the cursor around on the screen until it was afternoon. Then I'd go tutor my students as darkness engulfed the road outside for the day.

I walked to the river on my way back home as always, the railroad to my side. The busy road was crossed as I prayed with all I could to not end up a roadkill. Passing the entry row to the trawler dock left my mood strained. Students were supposed to pass for free and yet I'd be bickering with the guards on weekends to go through.

The boat was crowded today, surprisingly. A rare sight for a noon and a taste of death on a disgustingly hot day. I stared at the mild waves, trying to forget the agonizing heat. Over to the other side of the boat.

And there she was.

But this time our eyes didn't meet. I didn't know what they were fixated on, the river or the empty void I always stare into.

The pretend detective in me didn't sense an alarm. I was one of the very few students who went to my college over from this side. But she was not the only one apart from me. There was another female student on the boat if anything, who I'd see quite regularly.

As we crossed onto the other side, I climbed up to the dock. The woman was somehow ahead of me.

From here on, I took an auto to my house. They park up ahead, while the CNGs are to the right. She walked as fast as I'd some other day if I wasn't wishing to stay behind her. For a while, she hesitated to choose between the two. I smiled. *Been there, done that.*

The auto was cheaper, but the 5 seconds of walk to get there sometimes proved too much. As opposed to just getting into the CNG and waiting in peace until it filled up.

That's where we'd part. Maybe I would see her again in the campus tomorrow. I hoped I did.

20 minutes away from resting my body, I imagined myself letting off steam and sit in front of the desktop. After a little rest, get some work done on it.

After a while, she was no longer all there was in my thoughts.

___

I paid the fare and stepped out of the vehicle.

The house was a little further in from the road. The fence gate would look locked from a distance, but closing in put an end to that illusion. But I had the key in my hand regardless.

The gate was in fact, unlocked. My sister had already come home, as it seemed. I slipped out the shackle and looked at the door.

I almost dropped the key.

The woman from my classroom.

She stood across from me.
